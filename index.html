Margin note

Roughly 90,000 small law firms, conveyancers, accountants and agencies became reporting entities on 1 July 2026, and came under the Privacy Act the same day.

The obligation that catches practices is not the checking. It is the proof that you did not keep what you checked.

1

The bind, set out as clauses

Open any clause for the plain English version. This is how the obligation actually lands in a practice of your size.

1.1

From 1 July 2026 a captured practice must verify the identity of its client before acting.

Nobody argues with this one. You already ask for the licence, and your AML software already records the check.
1.2

The practice must not retain the identity document once the verification is recorded.

This is the one your office is built to break. Save to matter, scan to folder, reply with attachment, back up nightly.
1.3

The practice must be able to demonstrate that it is not retaining identity documents.

Proving a negative, across every mailbox and drive you own, without an inventory and without an IT person to build one.

2.2

Penalties reach $33 million for a body corporate, with parallel exposure under the Australian Privacy Principles over the same data.

A breach of client identity records hits your regulator, your insurer and your clients on the same morning. The aggravating fact is that the data should never have been there.
